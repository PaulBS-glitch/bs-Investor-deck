03 / Market failure

Waiting, LMI and family support do not solve the deposit gap properly.

Existing pathways either delay ownership, increase leverage, depend on unequal family support, or force poor property compromise.

The deposit gap is usually treated as a personal savings problem. In practice, the available alternatives are inefficient, uneven or structurally limited. That creates room for a governed contribution-layer solution that sits beside, rather than replaces, senior-lender underwriting.

01

Waiting

Continuing to save can extend rent leakage and leave the buyer exposed to further price movement before they can transact.

Delays ownership rather than solving the capital stack.
02

High-LVR / LMI pathway

LMI may help some buyers transact, but it increases debt burden, adds cost and remains dependent on senior-lender appetite.

Uses more leverage, not a distinct contribution layer.
03

Family support

Parental assistance is uneven, informal and not scalable. It helps some buyers but does not create a market-wide solution.

Access depends on household wealth, not borrower quality.
04

Buying down / compromising

Buying a cheaper or less suitable property may solve the deposit issue but can force poor property selection or unsuitable housing outcomes.

May weaken asset quality and customer fit.
Institutional takeaway: the market needs a structured, governed contribution-layer solution that allows eligible buyers to transact earlier without replacing senior-lender underwriting.
