03 / Market failure

The alternatives do not convert serviceable demand into transactions.

Why existing options fail

Waiting, LMI, family support and buying down may help individual buyers, but they do not create a scalable contribution-layer solution.

Market gap

Brokers already see customers who can support a mainstream home loan but cannot bridge the deposit gap quickly enough. The result is delayed ownership, lost transactions or buyers compromising on the wrong property.

01 / Waiting

The buyer remains outside ownership.

Rent continues while the buyer saves into a moving property market.

Failure point: delays the transaction rather than solving the capital stack.
02 / High-LVR or LMI

The solution is more leverage.

Some buyers may transact, but with higher costs, higher debt and ongoing senior-lender appetite constraints.

Failure point: increases senior debt rather than adding a separate contribution layer.
03 / Family support

Access depends on family wealth.

Parental assistance can solve the gap for some buyers, but it is uneven, informal and not available to all suitable borrowers.

Failure point: household wealth determines access, not borrower quality.
04 / Buying down

The buyer compromises the asset.

A cheaper property may reduce the deposit hurdle, but can force location, quality or long-term suitability trade-offs.

Failure point: solves deposit maths while weakening asset quality and customer fit.
Institutional takeaway: the market failure is unconverted, mortgage-capable demand. BuySooner converts that demand into governed contribution-layer transactions while preserving senior-lender underwriting.
