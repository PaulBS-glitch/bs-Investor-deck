03 / Market failure

Existing pathways do not turn deposit-constrained demand into controlled transactions.

Waiting, high-LVR lending, family assistance and property compromise may help some buyers, but they do not create a structured contribution-layer solution that preserves senior-lender underwriting.

Market gap

Brokers already see customers who can support a mainstream home loan but cannot bridge the deposit gap quickly enough. The usual result is a delayed transaction, a lost deal or a customer who walks away.

01

Waiting

The buyer keeps paying rent while trying to save into a moving property market.

Failure point: delays the transaction rather than solving the capital stack.
02

High-LVR / LMI

More leverage may help some buyers transact, but it adds cost, increases borrower leverage and remains subject to lender appetite.

Failure point: uses more senior debt, not a separate contribution layer.
03

Family support

Parental assistance can solve the gap for some buyers, but it is uneven, informal and not available to all otherwise suitable borrowers.

Failure point: access depends on family wealth, not borrower quality.
04

Buying down / compromising

A cheaper or less suitable property may reduce the deposit hurdle, but it can force poor asset selection or customer-fit compromise.

Failure point: may solve deposit maths while weakening property quality.
Institutional takeaway: the market failure is unconverted, mortgage-capable demand. BuySooner converts that demand into governed contribution-layer transactions while preserving senior-lender underwriting.
