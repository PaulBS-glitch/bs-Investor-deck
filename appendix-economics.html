Appendix 5 / Illustrative economics and investor return profile

Single-transaction economics show the gross return pathway.

The example below starts with a $1 million transaction so the economics are clear before bridging back to portfolio, facility and investor waterfall terms.

Illustrative transaction

$1 million property, 10% BuySooner contribution.

Senior mortgage$800,000
Customer equity$100,000
BuySooner contribution$100,000

If the property exits at $1.25 million after three years, the formula produces a gross BuySooner exit amount made up of capital returned plus formula participation.

$100,000 + ($1,250,000 × 2% × 3) = $175,000

Investor interpretation

The example shows how a single contribution-layer position can generate capital return plus formula participation. It is not a promised investor return. Actual investor outcome depends on facility pricing, funding costs, portfolio performance, Stage 2 utilisation, recovery timing and final waterfall terms.

Appendix takeaway: the $1 million example explains the unit economics. The investor case is created by repeating controlled contribution-layer positions across a diversified portfolio, with disciplined origination, liquidity coverage, recovery controls and institutional reporting.
