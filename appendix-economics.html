Appendix 6 / Illustrative economics and investor return profile

Single-transaction economics explain the return pathway.

The example starts with a $1 million transaction so the unit economics are clear before moving to portfolio, facility and investor waterfall terms.

Illustrative transaction

$1 million property, 10% BuySooner contribution.

Senior mortgage$800,000
Customer equity$100,000
BuySooner contribution$100,000

Base case: 4.5% annual property growth over 3 years.

Exit value: ~$1.141m.

Formula participation: $1.141m × 2% × 3 = ~$68.5k.

Exit amount: $100k + ~$68.5k = ~$168.5k

Investor interpretation

This shows transaction-level economics only, using the 4.5% annual-growth base case from the formula sensitivity. It is not a promised investor return. Actual investor outcome depends on facility pricing, funding costs, portfolio performance, losses, Stage 2 utilisation and final waterfall terms.

Appendix takeaway: the $1 million example shows how one contribution-layer position produces capital recovery plus formula participation. Investor outcomes depend on portfolio quality, exit timing, funding costs, losses, liquidity controls and final waterfall terms.
