Appendix 4 / Portfolio construction and concentration controls

Portfolio risk is controlled by the shape of the pool, not only by individual approvals.

BuySooner monitors concentration by geography, property type, contribution band, origination vintage and expected exit timing, with authority to tighten settings as risk indicators change.

01

Geography and postcode clustering

Monitor state, metro area, postcode and local market concentration so exposure does not accumulate in a narrow property market.

02

Property type and liquidity

Control exposure to houses, townhouses and apartments, and restrict non-standard, illiquid or oversupplied property segments.

03

Contribution band mix

Track exposure across Core Retail, Elevated Retail, Structured and Exception bands so higher-contribution files do not dominate the pool.

04

Vintage and exit timing

Monitor settlement cohorts, Year 3 review dates, Year 5 endpoint risk and potential refinance or sale-exit clustering.

Management actions

Portfolio indicators should change origination behaviour.

The control is not just reporting. Credit and management must be able to tighten criteria before concentration becomes capital risk.

Geographic clustering

Restrict locations or slow origination.

High contribution drift

Reduce limits or require escalation.

Property-type concentration

Restrict higher-risk property types.

Exit timing cluster

Stagger originations or prioritise exits.

Weakening refinance conditions

Tighten assumptions or pause marginal deals.

Portfolio discipline

Concentration is an approval and monitoring issue.

The same credit settings that approve transactions must also govern the composition and risk profile of the portfolio.

Appendix takeaway: portfolio risk is managed through concentration monitoring, contribution-band discipline, property liquidity controls, vintage tracking and authority to tighten origination settings before risk becomes capital impairment.
